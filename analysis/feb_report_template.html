Daily steps per user vs canteen revenue — February 2026

Steps: mean of per-user daily totals, valid activity rows. Revenue: student traffic only (test terminals, maintenance flows, >$200 excluded).

School days are the active days. Students average ≈8,100 steps on a school day vs ≈3,600 on non-school days (+124%). The wearable goes on with the uniform, too: ~7,800 users sync on a school day vs ~2,300 on weekends.
Chinese New Year shows up in both sensors. Revenue: $20K on CNY-eve half-day (Mon 16th, 4% of a normal Monday), then $0 on the 17th–18th. Steps sag the same way — CNY Day 2 (2,650) was the quietest weekday of the month, below a typical Sunday.
The return was soft. Back at school Thu 19th–Fri 20th, steps rebounded immediately (7,441 / 7,960) but spending stayed 12–27% below normal — canteens recovered a full week slower than activity did.

Canteen revenue by hour — school days

17 school days combined. 9–11am = $3.8M, 50.4% of all school-day revenue.

07:00 — the commute spike. The first movement peak of the day happens before the first meaningful spending hour; only $77K trades before 7am.
09:00–11:00 — recess. Movement stays elevated through the recess block while half the day's revenue clears. The two peaks are the same event, recorded by different hardware.
13:00 — the biggest movement of the day. The lunch/dismissal slot is the single largest activity spike (≈1,180 steps/hr) and the #2 revenue hour ($1.14M). After 15:00 spending dies but movement continues to 18:00 — CCA hours are step-rich and purchase-poor. Weekends echo it: Saturdays (4,178 steps) beat Sundays (2,521) by 66%, a morning-CCA signature.

The firmware fault predates May supplier-relevant

May's investigation traced all 255-bpm and sub-40-bpm sensor errors to firmware 1.1.9.0.0. February shows the same signature — the fault was already live a quarter earlier.

Reading the table

  • 373 whole-record 255 errors (lowest = highest = avg = 255) in February — 100% from firmware 1.1.9.0.0, which carries just 8.4% of HR volume. Identical pattern to May.
  • 176 sub-40-bpm rows — also 100% from 1.1.9.0.0.
  • A further 220 rows have highest_bpm = 255 with a real hourly average — a momentary ceiling spike inside an otherwise valid hour. These never enter average-HR calculations, which use avg_bpm only.
  • All 255/sub-40 rows are excluded from every average in this report by the 30–220 bpm validity gate.

Method & caveats

  • No individual linkage. Card IDs and wearable user IDs are different namespaces with no join key (studentId is 100% NULL). Every comparison is population-level on shared calendar days.
  • Populations differ in size. ~179K active cards vs ~13.6K wearable users — the wearable cohort is a subset of schools/students. Day-level co-movement is expected (same calendar), so the value is in where the two disagree (e.g. the soft post-CNY spending return while activity snapped back).
  • Validity gates (same as the main pipeline): activity rows need data_type ∈ {0,1}, ≤4,000 steps and ≤500 kcal per 30-min interval; HR rows need 30–220 bpm and a February payload date. Transactions exclude vendor test terminals (STYL), "Retrieve…" maintenance flows, and amounts >$200.
  • School days detected from the data: weekdays whose transaction volume exceeds 40% of the school-day median → 17 school days; Mon 16 Feb (CNY-eve half-day) classifies as non-school.
  • Units: amounts in cents SGD (÷100 shown), calories in millical (÷1000 → kcal), steps per 30-min incremental interval.
  • Heart-rate rows are hourly summaries keyed by sync time; the payload carries the true date/hour. bpm_number equals the payload's avg_bpm (verified row-for-row in the May audit), so hourly averages are means of device-computed hourly averages.
  • Pipeline: analysis/convert_parquet_feb.py → analysis/analyze_feb.py → feb_insights.json → this file. See PIPELINE.md.
