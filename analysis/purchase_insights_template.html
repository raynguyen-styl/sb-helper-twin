What 13 million purchases reveal

Purchase-behaviour analysis of the Feb–Apr 2026 canteen transactions. The data has no item names. A stall gets a food category only if its registered name says what it sells — 554 stalls, 18.5% of purchases. The other 2,074 stalls carry generic names ("Stall 1") and are reported as Unclassified: price facts only, never guessed (method). Vendor test terminals, maintenance flows and >$200 outliers are excluded.

What sells category mix

Share of purchases — the honest picture

81.5% of purchases happen at generic-named stalls and stay Unclassified (grey). Among the name-verified rest: cooked food leads, then drinks, then bookshops — but note the verified sample skews toward stall types that register real names (bookshops especially).

Verified vs Unclassified — what each really is

A category row exists only because the stall names say so (554 stalls). The Unclassified row is everything we refuse to guess: 2,074 stalls, median charge $1.50, 71% of purchases ≤$2.

The purchase clock time patterns

Category share through the school day — verified stalls

Shares within name-verified volume. Before school it's drinks + snacks (58% at 6 am); cooked food peaks at the 12 pm lunch (43%). Only 15 snack stalls carry verified names, so the snack line is thin — treat it as "named snack stalls", not all snacking.

Bookshop sales follow the recess bell too

School bookshops sell at 9–10 am recess and the 1 pm break — not before school. Students treat stationery shopping as a recess activity.

Bookshop revenue spikes at term boundaries

Daily bookshop revenue: the biggest days are 2 Feb (term 1 resumption) and 31 Mar / 1 Apr (term 2 start after the March holidays) — back-to-school restocking. Canteen food shows no such seasonality.

The mix barely moves across weekdays — verified stalls

Verified category shares are stable Monday–Friday (drinks 28.4–28.9%) — no "Friday treat" effect. What students buy is set by the daily timetable, not the day of week.

Primary vs Secondary vs JC school levels

School level inferred from ID suffixes (…PS/…P → primary, …SS/…GS/…HS → secondary, …JC → junior college); 63 unmatched schools are grouped as "Other/Tertiary" and excluded from level comparisons.

The purchase clock shifts later with age

Share of each level's purchases by hour. Primary school activity is front-loaded (9–10 am recess = 59% of the day); JC students buy latest (11 am–12 pm peak). You can read the timetable from the payment data.

Average ticket by hour and level

Secondary students consistently pay ~45% more per purchase — and their ticket jumps after 8 am when full meals become available. (Category-free — computed over all transactions.)

Buying habits card-level behaviour

Purchases per day — older students stack more same-day buys

Share of card-days by number of purchases. Secondary students make 4+ purchases on 20% of their active days (vs 13% for primary) — more freedom, more spending money, longer hours on campus.

Same-day purchases come in bursts

Gap between a card's consecutive purchases on the same day: 59% happen within 10 minutes — the queue-again-for-a-drink pattern within one recess, not spread across the day.

The classic combo: food, then drink, 6 minutes later

Most common consecutive pairs on the same card & day within named stalls, with the median gap. Meal→drink is the signature combo (median 5.8 min); repeated drink purchases are the single most common pattern.

Students are loyal to one stall

For cards with ≥15 purchases, the favourite stall takes half of everything they buy — rising to 60% at JC (smaller canteens, stronger routines). Stall choice is habit, not exploration.

Reading the outliers

High-spend schools come in two shapes.

BRPS reaches $6.84/student-day with only 1.26 purchases/day — a $6.10 average ticket driven by bookshop/supplies sales, not food. WLDSS reaches $6.76 the opposite way: 2.62 cheap purchases a day. Same headline number, completely different canteen economies — frequency and ticket size must be read together.

Named-stall food prices rise with level.

Median cooked-food price at named stalls: $1.80 primary → $2.40 JC → $2.80 secondary (JC sample is small: 3.3K purchases). An earlier version claimed JC food was cheaper — that came from price-inferred stalls and did not survive the switch to verified-only data.

Method & caveats

Classification policy (no SKU data exists):
① A stall gets a category only if its registered name says what it sells — keyword match on the stall name (drinks / snacks & desserts / cooked food / fruits / bookshop & supplies). 554 stalls qualify: 18.5% of purchases, 19.5% of revenue.
② The 2,074 generic-named stalls ("Stall N") are Unclassified — described by factual price statistics only, never assigned a food type.
③ An earlier price-profile clustering step (k-means guessing categories from price patterns) was removed at the service team's request: an estimate, however plausible, must not read as fact.
Limits to keep in mind:
· The verified sample is not a random sample: bookshops and drink stalls register descriptive names far more often than generic canteen stalls, so verified shares describe named stalls only.
· Category-free analyses (timetables, spend per level, loyalty, purchase bursts, school scatter) still use all 13.4M transactions — only the food-type labels are restricted to named stalls.
· School level comes from ID-suffix inference; 63 schools ("Other/Tertiary", incl. NYP, SOTA) are excluded from level comparisons.
· Per-card stats cover the 73% of transactions with a cardId (bank-card taps are anonymous).
· Excluded as non-student traffic: STYL test terminals, "Retrieve…" maintenance flows, amounts >$200.
· Fix at source: have stall operators register descriptive names — every named stall becomes verifiable.
